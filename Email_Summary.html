Carbon Credits
Carbon Credits, Not Carbon Prices: Why CCTS May Not Clear CBAM's Article 9 Bar

29 Jul 2026 · LiveLaw

Overview

LiveLaw discusses why India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS) may not satisfy the carbon price requirements under Article 9 of the EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM). The analysis suggests that while CCTS involves credits, it might not be recognized as an explicit carbon price equivalent by the EU, potentially impacting the deductions Indian exporters can claim. This assessment is crucial for understanding the financial implications for Indian goods entering the EU market under CBAM rules.

India Impact  Indian firms exporting to the EU, particularly those covered by CBAM, face potential increased costs if CCTS credits are not recognized as a deduction, necessitating a clear understanding of the regulatory interaction.

 
EU ETS
EU ETS: Commission publishes proposal for the revised Emissions Trading System - Linklaters

28 Jul 2026 · Linklaters

Overview

The European Commission has published its proposal for the revised EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS), according to Linklaters. This revision aims to strengthen the EU's climate ambition by expanding the scope of the ETS to new sectors and tightening emission reduction targets. Key aspects include adjustments to free allocation rules, a higher linear reduction factor for emissions, and mechanisms to address carbon leakage, aligning with the EU's enhanced 2030 climate goals for greenhouse gas reductions.

India Impact  While primarily affecting EU operations, revisions to the EU ETS, particularly related to carbon leakage and potential linkages with CBAM, can indirectly influence global carbon pricing discussions and impact competitiveness for Indian industries interacting with the EU market.
