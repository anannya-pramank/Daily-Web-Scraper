India and Japan Adopt Rules of Implementation for Joint Crediting Mechanism Under Article 6.2 of Paris Agreement - PIB

16 Jun 2026 · PIB India

Overview

India and Japan adopted rules for implementing their Joint Crediting Mechanism (JCM) under Article 6.2 of the Paris Agreement. This mechanism enables both countries to collaborate on greenhouse gas emission reduction projects, allowing Japan to use the resulting credits towards its Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC) while contributing to sustainable development in India. The framework establishes the operational guidelines and procedures for project development and credit issuance.

India Impact  This development directly facilitates climate finance and technology transfer to India, creating opportunities for Indian businesses involved in emission reduction projects to generate and trade carbon credits, potentially boosting green investments and achieving national climate goals.

 
Carbon Border Adjustment
EU Council Backs Expansion of CBAM Carbon Import Tax to Finished Goods

15 Jun 2026 · ESG News

Overview

The Council of the European Union has agreed its position on strengthening the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), setting the stage for negotiations. This position supports the expansion of the CBAM carbon import tax to include finished goods, broadening its scope beyond initial raw materials. The move aims to tighten carbon border rules, ensuring that imported products bear a similar carbon cost to those produced within the EU, thereby preventing carbon leakage and promoting global climate action.

India Impact  This expansion of CBAM to finished goods significantly increases the compliance burden and potential cost for Indian exporters across various manufacturing sectors, necessitating comprehensive carbon footprint assessments and reporting for products destined for the EU market to avoid tariff imposition.

EU Member States Agree to Expand CBAM Carbon Import Tax to Downstream Products - ESG Today

15 Jun 2026 · ESG Today

Overview

EU Member States have agreed to expand the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) carbon import tax to cover downstream products. This decision reflects a consensus within the EU to broaden the scope of the mechanism, extending its reach beyond primary goods to more complex, manufactured items. The expansion is intended to further align import costs with internal carbon pricing, reinforce EU climate ambitions, and mitigate the risk of carbon leakage.

India Impact  This agreement by EU Member States to expand CBAM to downstream products intensifies the urgency for Indian manufacturers to assess and disclose embodied carbon in their exports, impacting competitiveness and requiring adjustments in supply chain practices to meet evolving EU trade standards.
