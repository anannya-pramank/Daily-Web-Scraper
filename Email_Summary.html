Turkey's Green Taxonomy paradox ahead of COP31: ‘Nuclear energy and fossil gas are unacceptable’

10 Oct 2026 · Bianet

Overview

Bianet highlights a significant "paradox" within Turkey's developing Green Taxonomy ahead of COP31, specifically concerning the contentious classification of both nuclear energy and fossil gas as acceptable sustainable activities. Critics deem this inclusion "unacceptable," pointing to a divergence from established international and EU taxonomy principles that typically exclude such energy sources from 'green' definitions. This classification decision is critical, impacting how future investments are labeled and channeled within Turkey’s national energy transition initiatives.

India Impact  India is also in the process of developing its own green taxonomy and has been considering the inclusion of natural gas and nuclear energy in its sustainable finance framework, offering a comparable context for classification debates.

The World Economic Forum identifies three pivotal factors set to define the next generation of data centres: enhanced sustainability, operational efficiency, and critical resilience. This underscores the escalating global demand for environmentally responsible and robust data infrastructure, driven by ongoing digital transformation across diverse industries.

India Impact  As India's digital economy experiences rapid expansion, the principles of sustainable and efficient data centre development highlighted by the WEF are crucial for Indian firms investing in or operating such infrastructure, influencing design, energy sourcing, and operational resilience.
