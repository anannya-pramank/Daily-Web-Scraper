Paris Agreement
India and Japan Adopt Rules of Implementation for Joint Crediting Mechanism Under Article 6.2 of Paris Agreement - PIB

16 Jun 2026 · PIB India

Overview

India and Japan have adopted Rules of Implementation for the Joint Crediting Mechanism (JCM) under Article 6.2 of the Paris Agreement, according to PIB India. This mechanism facilitates bilateral cooperation on greenhouse gas emission reductions and removals, allowing for the transfer of internationally transferred mitigation outcomes (ITMOs) between the two countries. The rules establish the operational framework and procedures for project development, validation, verification, and crediting within the JCM, promoting sustainable development and enhanced climate action through market-based approaches.

India Impact  This development creates a defined framework for Indian entities to participate in international carbon markets through bilateral projects with Japan, potentially attracting green investment and generating carbon credits.

Climate Risk
EBA Integrates Climate Risk into EU Banking Stress Test - ESG Today

16 Jun 2026 · ESG Today

Overview

The European Banking Authority (EBA) has integrated climate risk considerations into its regular EU banking stress test framework. This move mandates that financial institutions under EBA's purview will now be assessed for their resilience to various climate-related scenarios, including physical risks and transition risks. The integration aims to evaluate banks' preparedness for potential financial losses stemming from climate change and associated policy shifts. This ensures that climate risk management becomes a central component of prudential supervision, influencing capital adequacy and risk management strategies across the EU banking sector.

India Impact  This development in EU banking supervision highlights an intensifying global trend, suggesting that Indian banks and the Reserve Bank of India may face increasing pressure to formalize and integrate climate risk into their own stress testing and regulatory frameworks.

Environmental NGOs Challenge EU Carbon Removal Rules Over Bio-CCS, Biochar Integrity

16 Jun 2026 · ESG News

Overview

A coalition of environmental NGOs has escalated a formal challenge to the European Union's carbon removal rules. The NGOs express concerns over the scientific integrity of the EU's carbon removal framework, specifically targeting provisions related to Bio-CCS (Bioenergy with Carbon Capture and Storage) and biochar. They have questioned the methodology and robustness of these proposed carbon removal pathways, arguing that current rules may overstate their climate benefits or pose environmental risks. This challenge seeks to ensure stricter standards and greater scientific rigor in the EU's approach to carbon removal credits.

India Impact  Potential shifts in EU carbon removal standards could influence the credibility and market acceptance of similar carbon credit methodologies, indirectly affecting the design of future carbon market frameworks in India.
